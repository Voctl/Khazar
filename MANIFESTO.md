# THE KHAZAR MANIFESTO

> *A spectre is haunting the software industry — the spectre of freedom. All the powers of old Silicon Valley have entered into a holy alliance to exorcise this spectre: Apple and Microsoft, Oracle and Google, cloud vendors and DRM peddlers. It is high time that the free software community should openly, in the face of the whole world, publish our views, our aims, our tendencies.*

### ARTICLE I. THE RIGHT TO READ

You have the right to read every line of code running on your machine. Distributing binary blobs without source isnt a business decision — its violence. A system you cant audit is a system thats already compromised. **Your EULA is toilet paper.**

**Richard Stallman** proved this in 1983 when he quit MIT to build a free OS from scratch. No pay, no VC, just principle. He gave us GCC, Emacs, the GPL, and the word *copyleft*. Dude was on a mission fr.

### ARTICLE II. THE RIGHT TO MODIFY

Software isnt a product to consume, its speech to engage with. "You may look but not touch" makes you a subject, not a citizen. When a project betrays its community, the community doesnt beg — it **forks**.

**Linus Torvalds** proved it at planetary scale. A Finnish student wanted to understand his hardware, so he wrote a kernel. Now Linux runs 100% of the top 500 supercomputers, built by volunteers who never signed an NDA. Absolute madman.

<br clear="all">

### ARTICLE III. THE RIGHT TO SHARE

Knowledge you cant share is knowledge that dies. Hoarding code behind NDAs and trade secrets is burning the Library of Alexandria, one repository at a time.

The cathedral builders say softwares too complex for you — you need their priests, their certs, their support contracts. **Lies.** The bazaar proved a thousand eyes catch every bug. **Eric S. Raymond** was right: closed development isnt just immoral, its inefficient. The cathedrals a mausoleum. The bazaars alive. And its way more fun tbh.

<br clear="all">

### ARTICLE IV. THE RIGHT TO REINVENT

Dependency cultures a sickness. `npm install` pulls down eight hundred thousand files from strangers, code youll never read. Your hello world weighs 200 MB. Youve built nothing and understood less. Thats not a flex, thats a problem.

Build it yourself. Write the parser. Allocate the memory. Handle the interrupt. Strip the abstractions until its just you and the silicon. Coding for the joy of understanding is what built UNIX — and its what built Khazar.

<br clear="all">

### ARTICLE V. THE COVENANT OF C

C isnt a language, its a **covenant** between you and the machine. It doesnt hold your hand. It assumes youre an adult who can manage your own memory, and it rewards you with power no managed language ever will.

**Dennis Ritchie** gave us C and UNIX. No patents, no incorporation, just code — and every OS youve ever used traces back to him. We honor that not with monuments but with `malloc`, `memcpy`, and inline assembly. We read the Intel manual. We write the kernel. **We carry the covenant forward.** RIP to a real one.
